# Visual continuity — keeping the eye hooked

## Focal zone

- Keep focal points in a stable zone — in vertical references mostly a central column, upper third to middle. Across 16 shots the eye barely moves sideways.
- Captions sit in one consistent spot for the whole video (centre or chest height).

## Continuity map (Phase 4)

For every shot record the focal point at **entry** and **exit** (rough grid position, e.g. "centre-top", "lower-left third").
- Shot B's entry focal point = shot A's exit focal point.
- Transitions start where the eye already is: the circle wipe grows from the Moon's position; the iris opens from the dot where the text sat; the Moon shrinks to exactly where the next shot's Moon appears.
- If a jump is unavoidable, lead the eye with motion (a whip in the direction of the new focal point) or a flash.

## Motif and shape rhymes

- Pick one motif per video (an object or a shape) and let it reappear in different forms: photo, drawing, texture, backdrop, UI element, transition matte.
- Shapes rhyme across cuts: circle → circle, rectangle → screen → card, line → horizon → progress bar. The shape survives even when the scene changes.

## Colour as a pointer

- One accent colour on a muted frame tells the eye where to look (a yellow folder, a red figure in a grey crowd, a green bin).
- Accent can be brief (a 1-second highlight that fades back) or persistent (the motif).
- Keep colour meanings consistent within a video (decided in the visual bible).

## Deliberate breaks

The one allowed break in flow is intentional: a cut to black with silence, a sudden hard cut on an impact. It works because everything else flows. Use at most one or two per video.

## Text and image relationship

- Words lead, pictures follow — but never more than a beat apart.
- The hero word and the hero image share colour or position when possible.
- Clear old text before new ideas arrive (clear-the-stage).
