# Asset roles and the era dial

Professional reference edits never use random assets. Each object fills a role in the story. The roles transfer to any topic; the objects never do. Fill every role fresh for each video, from that story's world.

## The seven roles

| Role | Job | Example from references (do not reuse as-is) |
|---|---|---|
| **World** | Says *when and where* in under a second | 1950s uniforms, lab coats, oscilloscopes, desk lamps |
| **Motif** | One shape/object that recurs in new forms and powers transitions; the viewer's anchor | The Moon as photo, blueprint, newspaper picture, surface texture, backdrop, then a circle wipe |
| **Proof** | Makes an unbelievable claim feel real | Newspapers, patent drawings, news screenshots, handwritten notes, real archival footage |
| **Danger / Stakes** | Makes risk or tension visible | Nuclear rocket, radiation symbol, bomb card, fraud alert — usually the accent colour |
| **Surfaces** | Text sits on a material from the story's world, not flat colour | Lunar surface, wooden desk, grainy paper, patent paper, blueprint grid |
| **Bridge to CTA** | Moves from story to CTA without leaving the world | Retro computer for a 1950s story; slot machine for "that's the bet"; desk with laptop for a modern story |
| **Colour roles** | Each colour has one job | Red = danger/emphasis, orange = absurd twist, blue = calm tech, green = "do this now" |

Two more devices that recur:
- **Number → object.** Abstract figures become physical things that do something: revenue = a building rising through clouds; a sale = a "for sale" shop with a price sign; valuation = a hand stacking money cubes; a bet = a slot machine. For every number in the script, ask "what object is this, and what does it do?"
- **Literal metaphor transitions.** A rejected idea = the drawing crumples into a ball and drops into a bin. Years passing = a page of years counting up.

## The era dial

Era is a dial, not a theme. Not every video goes vintage. Choose per story:

- **Pure past** — a story set in history (the A119 Moon plan).
- **Pure present** — current tech news, how something works today (Stripe buying OpenRouter).
- **Pure future** — speculative tech.
- **Mash-up** — old story, modern payoff (Hedy Lamarr → your Wi-Fi). Often the best fit for tech history, because old ideas literally live inside modern devices. The bridge moments are where the eras touch: a modern text-selection cursor on vintage patent paper, macOS-style labels on radar rings.

Frame rate can follow the era: 24 fps for a film-era feel, 30 fps default, 60 for a hyper-modern UI feel.

### How each role changes with era

| Role | Past / disappeared tech | Present | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| World | Period rooms, period devices, period clothing | Real streets, real devices in hand, offices, data centres | Clean rooms, labs, cool high-key light |
| Motif | A signature part of the old tech (a floppy shutter, a game pixel, a dial) | The data packet, the QR square, the notification | A light filament, a neuron, a wave |
| Proof | Magazine pages, print ads, archival footage, patents, boxed products | Real screenshots, official data, charts, news sites, tweets | Research paper pages, patent drawings, prototype photos |
| Stakes | Shutdown notice, dying LED, rejection letter | Fraud alert, failed payment, outage screen | Warning label, red system alert |
| Surfaces | Beige plastic, LCD pixel grid, VHS-noise card, old paper | Phone glass, brushed aluminium, circuit board, desk | Holographic glass, white composite, light grid |
| Grade | Warm, low contrast, digicam/VHS/halftone texture, or B&W | Natural, crisp | Cool, clean, bloomed highlights |

Proof changes form the most: newspapers for the past, screenshots and data for the present, papers and patents for the future.

## Grade as storytelling

Colour can carry the timeline or the emotion:
- Grey/B&W = past, struggle, the "before"; colour arrives with success or "today".
- One accent colour on an otherwise grey frame marks the object the story is about (and where to look). Use it briefly as a pointer, or persistently as the motif.
- A section's grade can shift slowly (colour → B&W to signal "archive").

Decide colour meanings in the visual bible and keep them consistent within the video.

## Real vs generated

- Use real material where accuracy matters: data, UI, screen recordings, product shots, real people's actual footage (with rights).
- Use AI imagery for atmosphere, worlds, metaphors and reconstructions.
- Never generate fake versions of real publications, logos-as-articles or real people's statements presented as genuine. Invent mastheads, or use real archives with credit.
